FINANCE GLOSSARY

Commercial finance, explained.

Clear definitions of common funding and lending terms.

Amortisation

The gradual repayment of borrowing through scheduled payments over an agreed period.

Arrangement Fee

A fee charged for setting up a finance facility. It may be paid upfront, deducted from proceeds or added to the facility, depending on the lender.

Asset Finance

Finance used to acquire or refinance business assets such as vehicles, machinery, equipment and technology.

Asset Refinance

Funding raised against assets a business already owns, allowing capital tied up in those assets to be released.

Asset-Based Lending (ABL)

Finance supported by business assets such as receivables, stock, plant, machinery or property.

Balloon Payment

A larger final payment due at the end of some finance agreements after lower scheduled payments during the term.

Base Rate

A benchmark interest rate that may be used when calculating the interest payable on variable-rate finance.

Bridging Finance

Short-term secured finance commonly used for property purchases, auctions, refurbishment or other time-sensitive transactions.

Business Loan

A facility providing capital to a business, normally repaid over an agreed term together with interest and any applicable fees.

Capital Expenditure (CapEx)

Money spent by a business on longer-term assets such as equipment, vehicles, machinery or property.

Cash Flow

The movement of money into and out of a business over a period of time.

Cash Flow Forecast

A projection of expected cash receipts and payments, used to understand future liquidity and funding requirements.

Commercial Mortgage

Longer-term borrowing secured against commercial property, commonly used to purchase or refinance business or investment premises.

Covenant

A condition within a finance agreement requiring the borrower to meet specified financial or operational requirements.

Credit Limit

The maximum amount available to borrow under a credit facility, subject to its terms and continuing eligibility.

Debenture

A form of security that can give a lender rights over specified company assets and, depending on its terms, may include fixed and floating charges.

Debt Consolidation

Combining or restructuring existing borrowing into a new facility or smaller number of facilities.

Debt Service

The payments required to meet interest, capital and other scheduled obligations on borrowing.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

A measure comparing cash available for debt service with the debt repayments that must be made.

Development Finance

Funding for property development projects, often released in stages against land or property value and build progress.

Director's Guarantee

A personal guarantee provided by a company director in support of specified business borrowing.

Drawdown

The act of taking some or all of the funds available under an agreed finance facility.

EBITDA

Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a commonly used measure of operating performance before those items.

Equity

The ownership value in a business or asset after relevant liabilities are deducted.

Exit Strategy

The planned method of repaying a short-term facility, for example through sale, refinance or another defined source of funds.

Factoring

A form of invoice finance that can combine funding against receivables with credit control and collections services.

Facility

An agreed arrangement under which a lender makes finance available subject to stated terms and conditions.

Facility Letter

A document setting out the principal terms and conditions of an offered finance facility.

Finance Lease

An arrangement allowing a business to use an asset in return for agreed rental payments over a set period.

Fixed Charge

Security taken over a specific asset, generally restricting disposal of that asset without the lender's consent.

Fixed Rate

An interest rate that remains unchanged for an agreed period, subject to the terms of the facility.

Floating Charge

Security over a class of changing business assets that may continue to be dealt with in the ordinary course of business until specified events occur.

Gross Profit

Revenue less the direct cost of producing or purchasing the goods or services sold.

Hire Purchase (HP)

Asset finance where the cost is spread over an agreed term and ownership typically transfers after all required payments and conditions are satisfied.

HMO

House in Multiple Occupation; a residential property occupied by multiple people or households and subject to relevant property and licensing rules.

Interest

The cost charged for borrowing money, normally expressed as a rate and calculated according to the facility terms.

Interest-Only

A repayment structure where scheduled payments cover interest while the capital is repaid separately, often at the end of the term.

Invoice Discounting

Invoice finance where funding is advanced against receivables and the business generally retains responsibility for collecting customer payments.

Invoice Finance

Funding that releases cash against eligible unpaid customer invoices.

Loan-to-Cost (LTC)

The amount of finance expressed as a percentage of the total cost of a project or transaction.

Loan-to-Value (LTV)

The amount borrowed expressed as a percentage of the value of the asset or property supporting the facility.

Management Buy-In (MBI)

A transaction where an external management team acquires control or ownership of an existing business.

Management Buyout (MBO)

A transaction where an existing management team acquires control or ownership of the business it manages.

Net Profit

Profit remaining after relevant business costs, expenses, interest and taxes are accounted for.

Overdraft

A bank facility allowing an account to operate below zero up to an agreed limit, subject to the bank's terms.

Personal Guarantee (PG)

A commitment from an individual to meet specified business obligations if the business does not, subject to the terms of the guarantee.

Purchase Order Finance

Funding designed to help meet supplier or production costs associated with fulfilling confirmed customer orders.

Receivables

Amounts owed to a business by customers, often represented by unpaid sales invoices.

Refinance

Replacing or restructuring existing finance with a new facility.

Repayment Profile

The timing and amount of payments required over the life of a finance facility.

Revolving Credit Facility (RCF)

A pre-agreed credit limit that may be drawn, repaid and potentially redrawn during the facility term, subject to lender terms.

Security

Assets or rights a lender may take in support of a finance facility.

Secured Loan

Borrowing supported by specified assets or security that the lender may have rights against if obligations are not met.

Selective Invoice Finance

Invoice finance that allows funding to be raised against selected eligible invoices rather than the whole sales ledger.

Term

The agreed period for which a finance facility is provided.

Term Loan

Borrowing provided for an agreed period with a defined repayment structure.

Trade Finance

Funding supporting the purchase or movement of goods, often bridging the timing gap between paying suppliers and receiving customer payment.

Unsecured Loan

Borrowing not secured against a specific business asset, although other lender requirements such as guarantees may still apply.

Variable Rate

An interest rate that can change during the facility term, often by reference to a benchmark rate.

Working Capital

Funds available to support the day-to-day operating cycle of a business.
